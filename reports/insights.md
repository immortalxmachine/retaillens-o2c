# RetailLens - Order-to-Cash Insights (SYNTHETIC DATA)

> Generated by `src/insights.py` from the KPI tables of the latest pipeline run. The data is synthetic (seeded generator), so the findings demonstrate the method, not a real company.

**Scope:** 12,488 sales orders, 32,122 order items, EUR 17.32M net revenue, 2023-01-01 to 2024-12-31. **Headline:** OTIF 74.0%, average lead time 5.6 days, fill rate 98.9%, return rate 4.9%, invoice-to-cash 38.1 days.

## 1. IT misses OTIF far more often than any other market
**Evidence:** OTIF in IT is 57.8% vs 83.2% in DE (overall 74.0%). In-full is 96.6% but on-time only 60.1%, so the gap is late shipments; average lead time is 7.5 days vs 4.6.

**Recommendation:** Review the outbound route and carrier SLA for IT, and align the requested delivery dates (VDATU) with the real transit time - or serve IT from a closer stock point.

## 2. Dresses drive returns - mostly a size/fit question
**Evidence:** Dresses return 9.5% of billed units (company average 4.9%), worth EUR 244,601 in returned value. 'Size / fit' explains 50.4% of the returned units in this category. Footwear follow at 7.3%.

**Recommendation:** Investigate dresses returns first: audit size charts and grading of the top-returned styles, add fit notes to the B2B catalogue, and track return rate per style as a buying KPI.

## 3. Customers in IT pay 9 days slower
**Evidence:** Invoice-to-cash averages 44.3 days in IT vs 34.8 in DE (overall 38.1); 33.8% of its paid invoices took more than 45 days. Open receivables there: EUR 145,061.

**Recommendation:** Tighten dunning for IT (reminder at due date + 7), review credit limits of the slowest payers, and consider an early-payment discount.

## 4. The festive peak brings the revenue - and the service problems
**Evidence:** November-December generate 27.4% of net revenue in 2 of 12 months, but OTIF falls to 61.2% in those months vs 78.2% the rest of the year. Revenue grew 20.0% year over year.

**Recommendation:** Plan warehouse and carrier capacity for the peak from October (temporary staff, pre-picking of bestsellers) and pull festive pre-orders forward with an early-order incentive.

## 5. Footwear availability causes the most split deliveries
**Evidence:** Fill rate for footwear is 98.2% vs 99.2% for outerwear, and 12.9% of its order items need more than one delivery (each extra delivery = extra picking, freight and an extra invoice).

**Recommendation:** Buy footwear by size curve per store cluster and hold safety stock on core sizes; use the split-delivery share as an allocation KPI.
